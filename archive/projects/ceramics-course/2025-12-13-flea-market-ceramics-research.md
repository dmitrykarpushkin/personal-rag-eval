Went to the Saturday flea market in Telliskivi with the mug from the course in my bag, to compare.

I turned over a dozen old cups and bowls and looked at the feet: where the glaze stops, how they sit, the little marks of the kiln stilts. One cheap bowl from the 1970s had a perfect foot, with a gentle bevel and a tiny recess. I held it for a long time and the seller said "take it, take it." I did.

Lessons:
- A thick, soft foot makes a pot look friendly
- A glaze that stops a few millimetres above the foot looks deliberate
- Old Estonian stoneware had very plain forms and almost always a brown glaze: honest and tough

I am a person who now buys bowls to read them.
